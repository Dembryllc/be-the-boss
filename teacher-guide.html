Be the Boss! — Teacher Guide

A 4th-grade ICT activity. Students invent a pretend business in five steps, then pitch it and print a certificate. Nothing is installed, nothing is submitted online, and no student data leaves the Chromebook.

Before the first lesson

  • Opening it: put the be-the-boss folder on Drive or any web address and give students the link, or open index.html straight from the device.
  • Use one address for the whole class. The app saves work against the exact address it was opened from. If some students use a link and others open the file, their saved work will not line up.
  • Check sign-in. If each student signs into the Chromebook with their own account, each gets their own business automatically. If devices are shared on one profile, read Collecting work below first.
  • No account, no login, no setup. There is nothing to create.

The one rule to say out loud

“Use a nickname. Never type your last name.” The app says it too, on the first screen. Nothing students type is sent anywhere, but the habit is the point.

The five steps

Each step is one screen with one skill. Roughly 25–35 minutes each, or run two in a longer block. Students can move back and forth freely — nothing is locked, on purpose.

StepThe skillWhat students doWatch for
1. Big Idea A business solves a problem for someone Choose Make It, Do It, or Fix It, then finish “My business sells ___ for ___ because ___.” “Because it’s cool” — push for a reason a customer would give.
2. Name & Logo Design with limits Name recipe (describing word + thing word), then a logo: one shape, one picture, up to three colours, one font. The three-colour cap is the lesson. The app refuses a fourth — ask them which one they are giving up and why.
3. Slogan Persuasive language Pick a trick (rhyme, same starting sound, command, question), write three slogans, tap a winner, see it on a poster. Writing only one and tapping it. Insist on three — comparing is the skill.
4. Customers Audience, and asking real people Fill in a customer card, then survey five classmates with Yes / No. The tally and bar update as they tap. Surveying friends who always say yes. Ask what a “no” would teach them.
5. Budget Money in, money out, profit $20 pretend cash, buy from the class price list, set a price and how many they will sell. Profit = Money In − Money Out. Pricing at $20 to “win”. Ask whether a classmate would really pay that.

Then: Pitch Day and the certificate

  • Pitch Day writes their pitch for them from their own answers, with a get-ready checklist and a one-minute timer. Good for partner practice before the real thing.
  • Certificate is a one-page printable with their logo, slogan, customer and profit. Print or save as PDF works either way.

The three levels

The toggle sits in the top right and is always visible. Change it any time, mid-step — nothing is lost. You can also set different levels on different devices for the same lesson.

Guided

Tap-to-choose word chips instead of typing, pre-filled choices, and a “Fill it in the easy way” button on the budget. For students who stall at a blank box.

On Track

Sentence starters appear as optional hints. The default for most of the class.

Stretch

Hints come off and a Challenge box appears on every step, including a “what if you sold half as many?” recalculation on the budget.

Read Aloud

Every card has a Read Aloud button that reads that screen using the Chromebook’s own voice. Headphones help. If a device has speech switched off, the button says so rather than hanging.

Collecting work

Work saves itself as students type — there is no Save button to hunt for, and a small green Saved flash confirms it. That save lives on that device, in that browser profile. It does not follow a student to a different Chromebook.

So the header has three buttons:

💾 SaveDownloads the whole business as one small file named after the student, for example captain-lemon-sunny-slime-shop-be-the-boss.json.
📂 OpenLoads that file back, on any computer.
↺ Start overErases the business on this device. It asks first.

To hand work in: have students tap Save, then drop the file into a Drive folder or a Classroom assignment. The filenames tell you whose is whose without opening any of them.

If the app is hosted as a web page

Where the app lives on a web page rather than in a folder, the browser will not let it hand over files or open the print window. It swaps those buttons by itself: Copy my work puts everything on the clipboard, so students paste it into a Classroom assignment or a document, and Paste my work brings it back. To keep a logo or poster, press and hold the picture and choose Save image. Everything else is the same.

Marking the whole class at once

Open teacher-review.html in a browser and drop the whole folder of files onto it. You get one table: who finished which steps, what they sell, their slogan, and their money in, out and profit. Sort by any column, open a student to see everything they wrote, print it, or download a CSV for your gradebook. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is kept when you close the tab.

If Chromebooks are shared

On a shared profile the app holds one business at a time, and the next student will overwrite the last one with no warning. End every lesson with “tap Save before you close the lid,” and start the next one with “tap Open and choose your file.” Treat it like handing out and collecting books.

Pictures they can keep

  • Logo — “Download logo (PNG)” in step 2. Good for a display board.
  • Poster — “Download poster (PNG)” in step 3.
  • Certificate — print it, or choose Save as PDF in the print window.

If something goes wrong

“My work is gone” Almost always a different device, a different browser profile, or a different web address than last time. If they saved a file, tap Open. If not, it is gone — the app keeps no copy anywhere else.
Two students, one device Each taps Save at the end and Open at the start. There is no second slot on one profile.
Nothing loads The app needs no internet at all — the font ships with it. If the page is blank, the folder was probably copied without its css, js or fonts folders. Copy the whole folder.
Animations are too much Turn on Reduce motion in Chromebook accessibility settings. The app follows it: no confetti, no sliding, everything still works.
A student is stuck on a blank box Switch them to Guided. They can tap words instead of typing.

Where it could go next

  • Market day. Print the posters and certificates, set up desks as stalls, and let students pitch to another class.
  • Real maths. Re-run step 5 with the actual cost of something you could make in class, and compare the guess to the real number.
  • Survey for real. Do the step 4 survey across two classes and talk about why the numbers differ.
  • Redesign round. After the pitches, give one round of feedback and let them change one thing — name, slogan, or price — and explain why.
